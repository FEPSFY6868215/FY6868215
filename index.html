How do we produce Hydrogen?

  

There are different types of hydrogen, the main ones being Green, Grey and Blue, we will be mainly focusing on Green, it is produced
by electrolysis from renewable sources involves breaking down water molecules (H2O) into oxygen (O2) and hydrogen (H2) (Iberdrola, 2022) .
This is the general overview but to delve further to first obtain Hydrogen there are three main steps: Molecular Transformation; Gasification
and Water Electrolysis. In the initial stages a high temperature water vapour is used to separate the carbon from the hydrogen that makes up the
natural gas  to obtain dihydrogen and carbon dioxide. Then in Gasification, the biomass is burned in a reactor at high temperatures
 (US Department of Energy, 2019) to make a desired yield. Finally, the products undergo water electrolysis to separate the hydrogen completely with the help 
of direct electrical current which connects to the anode and cathode via the water. 

   
How is green hydrogen Produced?

   

To specifically obtain Green hydrogen now, we use electrolysis which splits molecules into their constituent parts (Pilkington, 2022) therefore as we are using
water we obtain Hydrogen and oxygen at the cathode and anode respectively. In the electrolyser cell there are a pair of platinum electrodes immersed in the water, 
 (LibreTexts, 2016) platinum is used as it is chemically inert and will not produce side products during the process. Alongside this, an electrolyte is added usually 
such as sulfuric acid as pure water will not carry a high enough charge density. The yield of hydrogen produced from electrolysis is usually “about 70-80% of the energy
used to produce.”
